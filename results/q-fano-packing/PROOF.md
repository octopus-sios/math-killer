# At most 19 beta-flats in a binary hyperplane section

**Theorem.** Let $H=\mathbb F_2^6$ and let $K$ be a set of 3-spaces such that no 2-space is in two members and every point is in five members. There are at most 19 hyperplanes of $H$ whose contained members have a common point. Thus $|X_H|\ge44$ without the spanning hypothesis in Kurz, Theorem 6.10. No extension of $K$ to a design is assumed. Attainment of 19 is not asserted.

In a putative binary q-Fano plane this implies at least 92 non-alpha points, at most 35 alpha points, and at most 19 alpha points in any one hyperplane. Indeed apply Kurz, Lemma 6.11, with $t=44$: for $n=91$ the test fails ($36<g(17)=38$), and for $n=92$ it holds ($35\ge g(16)=33$).

**Attribution.** We use S. Kurz, *Alpha-points of a putative binary q-analog of the Fano plane*, arXiv:2611.30333v1, especially Sections 3–6. The earlier IIS report of Nov 28 (id 63f97639b44e) already gives 21 by excluding $t=9$. The argument here independently includes that case. The new exclusions are for 21 and 20, in *both* types of focal span. The finite lemmas below are checked by the accompanying standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` (integer and set operations only).

## 1. Reduction and the shadow bound

Use vector dimensions; a point denotes its unique nonzero binary vector. A line has three points and a plane has seven. By the section axioms there are 45 blocks, exactly five in every hyperplane, and two blocks meet in at most one point. A focal point $q$ belongs to precisely one beta-flat $G(q)$; all five blocks through $q$ lie in it and cover it (Kurz, Lemma 3.2). Write $A'$ for the set of focal points, in bijection with beta-flats.

Suppose $|A'|\ge20$. By Kurz, Theorem 6.10, $A'$ cannot span $H$; it spans a hyperplane $F$, since a solid contains only 15 points. By Kurz, Lemma 6.1, $F$ has an alternating form $f$ with one-dimensional radical $\langle r\rangle$, and for $q\in A'$ we have $q^\perp=G(q)\cap F$ (possibly all of $F$). We write $V=F/\langle r\rangle$, carrying the induced nondegenerate alternating form. In $F$ there are five inner blocks; the other 40 blocks each have a different **trace**, a line in $F$ not contained in an inner block. Each point $p\in F$ is on $5-j(p)$ traces, where $j(p)$ counts its inner blocks.

For an affine point $a\in H\setminus F$, the shadow $T(a)$ consists of the five traces of blocks through $a$, which are disjoint as point sets in $F$. For $p\in F$, let $O(p)$ consist of the affine points on blocks through $p$; thus $p\in T(a)$ iff $a\in O(p)$, where we identify the shadow with its union. The affine part of any outer block has four points. If $p$ is focal and $G(p)\ne F$, then $O(p)=G(p)\setminus F$. For the 15 nonzero fibers $\{p,p+r\}$ of $F\longrightarrow V$, set
$$
e_a=\#\{\{p,p+r\}\subseteq T(a)\}.
$$
Double counting gives $\sum_a e_a=\sum_{\{p,p+r\}} |O(p)\cap O(p+r)|$. We will bound each side even when traces are not rigid.

**Lower bound for a clean shadow.** Fix an isotropic spread $S$ of $V$. If none of the five traces through $a$ passes through $r$, their five projected lines are distinct (two different lifts of the same line meet in the plane over it). Each projected point occurs at most twice. The number of missing points of $V\setminus0$ equals the number $e_a$ of doubled points. The sum in $V$ of all projected points with multiplicities is zero, since each line sums to zero. The sum of all 15 distinct points is also zero, so $e_a=1$ is impossible: the unique missed point would equal the unique doubled one. If all five image lines are isotropic and outside $S$, $e_a$ cannot be zero either: no two isotropic spreads of $W(2)$ are disjoint (Kurz, Lemma on spreads of $W(2)$; verified directly in `flags()`). Thus $e_a\ge2$ for such a shadow.

More generally, if the lines of a shadow avoiding $r$ project to a spread avoiding $S$, at least **two** of them are non-isotropic. One is necessary as above; the number is even. For the latter assertion take a symplectic basis and the quadratic refinement $h(x)=x_1 x_2+x_3 x_4$. For a line $\{x,y,x+y\}$ the sum of its three $h$-values is $f(x,y)$ mod 2. Over a spread, these sums total zero, because each coordinate monomial occurs an even number of times on the 15 points. This also proves that a spread having four isotropic lines has five.

**Upper bound, fiber by fiber.** Suppose $p$ is focal with $G(p)\ne F$, and consider each outer block $B$ through $q=p+r$, with trace $L$. If $p\in L$, it contributes 4 points to the overlap, since $B\le G(p)$. Otherwise, if $L\subseteq p^\perp$, then $B\not\le G(p)$ (a contained block must pass through $p$), so $B\cap G(p)=L$ and it contributes zero. In the remaining case it contributes 2: its intersection with $G(p)$ is a line with exactly one point in $F$. Hence, in the absence of a trace through $r$, only non-isotropic traces of $q$ can contribute, 2 each.

For a pair neither of whose points is focal, consider all pairs of incident outer blocks, one at each point. Equal traces mean the same block and contribute 4. Distinct traces meeting inside $F$ contribute zero (the blocks already share a point); disjoint traces contribute at most 1. This upper bound needs only the traces, not their affine lifts.

## 2. Beta-flat span

If $F$ is a beta-flat, its focal point is $r$. The five inner blocks are the full preimages of a spread $S$ of lines of $V$. Write $X=F\setminus(A'\cup\{0\})$ and $t=31-|A'|\le11$. Each inner block containing a focal point other than $r$ projects to an isotropic line: if $q$ is such a point and $B$ is its inner block, then $B\subseteq q^\perp$ and its image contains the nonzero radical vector of the restriction to this image (namely the image of $q$). At most one inner block has all six points other than $r$ in $X$ ($t<12$). Hence at least four, thus all five, spread lines are isotropic.

Let $\Lambda$ consist of the 40 lines over the ten isotropic nonspread lines not containing $r$ (four lifts per line). All outer traces avoid $r$ and none projects to $S$. Every $p\ne r$ in $F$ lies on four members of $\Lambda$ (two lifts in each of the two isotropic nonspread planes over lines through its image), and on four traces. A trace through focal $p\ne r$ must lie in $p^\perp$ and hence be in $\Lambda$. Thus the four traces through $p$ are exactly its four $\Lambda$-lines.

If the traces are $\Lambda$, all 32 shadows are clean and $\sum e_a\ge64$. A fiber containing a focal point contributes zero to the upper bound; a fiber with neither focal contributes at most 8: each point has two traces in each of the same two planes over isotropic lines, and two traces in the same plane meet. Only the $2\cdot2+2\cdot2$ cross-plane pairs can contribute. There are at most $\lfloor11/2\rfloor$ such fibers, contradiction. This is Kurz's rigid-trace argument in the required range. It does not assume the trace-rigidity lemma for $t\ge9$.

Otherwise let $U$ be omitted $\Lambda$-lines and $W$ the traces replacing them. These sets have exactly the same degree at each point, and their common support lies in $X$. The members of $W$ project to non-isotropic lines. At any point of degree $d$ in $U$, the $2d$ lines through it in $U\cup W$ cover $1+4d$ points. Thus $d\le2$ since $t\le11$.

**Finite lemma B.** For fixed nondegenerate form and fixed isotropic spread on $V$, there are exactly 240 nonempty pairs $(U,W)$ satisfying those equalities and having support of size at most 11. All have $|U|=|W|=4$, with support **ten points on ten different nonzero fibers**. In particular no trade is possible at $t=9$.

**Verification.** Choose $r=16$ and use bits 0–3 for a symplectic basis of $V$ with paired positions $(0,1),(2,3)$. Any rank-four alternating form on $F$ is equivalent to this one by a choice of basis. Enumerate the 15 isotropic lines, and the 6 isotropic spreads as disjoint five-subsets. To justify a single spread, `flags()` explicitly traverses the flags $(S,m,s)$ (spread, member, point on member) by $x\mapsto x+f(x,v)v$, $v\in V\setminus0$. These linear involutions preserve the form. Its orbit is compared to the full enumerated set of 90 flags; images lift to $F$ fixing $r$. Alternatively `--all-flags` repeats the checks on every flag. The subroutine `trades()` considers subsets $U$ of the 40 lines in increasing index order, pruning if the support exceeds 11 or a point has degree three. It visits 129165 subsets including the empty one. A nonempty $U$ requires at least three different $W$-lines, one through each point on any chosen $U$-line. For every candidate $U$ of size at least three, enumerate all $|U|$-subsets of the 80 non-isotropic lifts contained in its support. Retain exactly those with identical degree masks; each retained pair is also compared by literal point-degree counters. This yields the stated histogram without IIS code or external tables.

So in the nonempty case at most one entire fiber is contained in $X$. Such a fiber contributes at most 16 because each point lies on just four outer blocks. All other fibers contribute at most $2\cdot 3|W|=24$ **in total** by the focal-fiber overlap bound (charge a non-isotropic trace for each incident point at most once). Thus $\sum e_a\le40$. On the other hand there are only $4|W|=16$ incidences of affine points with non-isotropic traces. A shadow with $e_a=0$ needs at least two such incidences; $e_a=1$ is impossible. Hence $\sum e_a\ge 2(32-8)=48$, contradiction.

## 3. Non-beta-flat span

Every focal point $q$ then lies on exactly one inner block (Kurz, Lemma 6.2): $q^\perp=F\cap G(q)$ is a solid of the beta-flat $G(q)$ through its focal point; its unique inner block passes through $q$. Equivalently the five blocks in $G(q)$ give a spread modulo $q$, and a plane in that quotient contains exactly one line of the spread. For any inner block $B$ each focal point of $B$ lies in the radical of $f|_B$. So **two or more focal points in $B$ force $B$ to be totally isotropic**, since the restriction has rank 0 or 2. A totally isotropic 3-space must contain $r$, or its image in the 4-dimensional symplectic quotient would have isotropic dimension 3.

The five possible patterns in Kurz, Proposition 4.1, have respectively 30, 23, 21, 18, 15 points of inner degree one. The first pattern is precisely the beta-flat case, and the last two cannot contain 20 focal points. In pattern $t_3$, only one or fewer of the 21 degree-one points could be nonfocal. Each block has at least three degree-one points (at most four intersection points with the other four blocks), hence at least two focal points. Then all five are totally isotropic and pass through $r$, contradiction.

In $t_2$, the four blocks through a common point have five degree-one points apiece, and at most three of the 23 degree-one points overall can be nonfocal. All four blocks are totally isotropic, so their common point is $r$. They are the full preimages of four disjoint isotropic lines of $V$. The three remaining points of $V$ sum to zero (sum over all points and sums of lines vanish), so they give a fifth line $m$ to complete a spread $S$. As above $m$ is isotropic.

The fifth inner block avoids $r$, and projects bijectively onto a plane $M$ of $V$. This plane cannot contain any of the four used spread lines (the fifth block would share a line with its preimage). A plane contains exactly one line of a spread, by counting its seven points: a spread member meets it in one or three points. Hence $m\subset M$. There is a unique $s\in m$ with $M=s^\perp$, since $m=m^\perp$, and $s$ spans the radical on $M$. The fifth block is the graph of a linear functional on $M$ in $F=V\oplus\langle r\rangle$. Subtracting an extension of this functional from the radical coordinate in $F$ does not change the other four blocks or the form. Hence we normalize this graph to $M$ with zero radical bit. Such a change of basis on $F$ extends to $H$.

The only possible focal point of the fifth block is now $s$. This leaves 21 eligible points: 20 degree-one points of the first four blocks plus $s$. Test each choice $A$ of 20 of the 21. This only assumes $A\subseteq A'$, so covers 21 as well as 20.

**Finite lemma N.** For each of these 21 choices there is precisely one set of traces satisfying the following necessary restrictions:
1. no trace lies in an inner block; through each $p\in F$ there are $5-j(p)$ traces;
2. for $p\in A$, its incident traces are exactly the four lines of $p^\perp$ through $p$ not in its unique inner block (there are $7-3=4$ such lines).

For all 21 choices, only two traces are exceptional (contain $r$, project to $S$, or are non-isotropic). The overlap upper bound of Section 1 is 28, 34, or 36, in 16, 4, or 1 cases, respectively.

**Verification.** Normalize the flag $(S,m,s)$ using the same exact 90-flag orbit check as before. `nonbeta()` constructs the five inner blocks, computes the 21 eligible points, and omits each one in turn. `trace_completions()` takes the union of forced lines from condition 2 and the candidate pool of other lines disjoint from $A$. It enumerates all ways to top up the forced union to 40 with candidates, then checks degrees at **every** point of $F$. For each surviving trace system, `shadow_bounds()` applies the fiber-by-fiber overlap bounds using only whether a fiber meets $A$ and whether two traces coincide, intersect or are disjoint; a point outside $A$ is never assumed focal. It also counts the exceptional traces. No SAT instance or rejected lift is being used as a certificate.

Since each exceptional trace contributes at most four affected affine points, at least $32-2\cdot4=24$ shadows are clean, giving $\sum e_a\ge48$. This contradicts even the largest upper bound 36. All cases are excluded, proving the theorem.

## Replay and integrity check

Run `python3 verify.py` from this directory. The finite lemmas do not rely on group, SAT or geometry software. `python3 verify.py --all-flags` repeats for all six spreads and all 90 marked flags. The default run also corrupts a balanced trade by replacing one of its four lines by another distinct eligible line; the pointwise checker must reject it. Running `python3 verify.py --mutate` feeds the corrupt trade directly to the checker and intentionally exits nonzero.
